# Launch campaign, creator kit, and operating system

## Series and promise

The Climb is a recurring documented experiment. Lead with a comprehensible real outcome, then show decisions, consequences, and a specific next attempt. Initial season is Road to the First Verified Win unless refreshed evidence makes that obsolete. Use one primary directed/edited program; optional individual model feeds are secondary, not required to understand the experiment.

The initial campaign is not a broad “we are the best agent benchmark” launch. Present the working experiment and invite useful interpretation. A maintainer-recorded defeat/replay can be interesting; publish only what rights and evidence permit. If capture access is absent, produce a clearly titled source-linked run report and fully build the video template without pretending it is a finished real gameplay episode.

## Required content package

Deliver 12 numbered weekly episode briefs, 24 numbered short-clip briefs (two per week), and 6 technical article outlines (one per two-week pair). These are templates and conditional assignments, not invented future findings. Every item needs audience, objective, truthful hook, source requirements, visual asset IDs, storyboard/structure, CTA, target destinations, rights/approval gate, owner, and expected measurement. Use relative week numbers until an actual launch date is authorized.

Write the first-episode script in two forms: actual-capture-ready and report-only. Include evidence-qualified title/description/thumbnail copy variants. Do not hard-code a model roster from an outdated ranking. Add a digest template, launch post variants, channel/profile bios, pinned community introduction, press boilerplate, one concise pitch, sponsor disclosure, and a corrections/update template.

## Suggested twelve-week editorial arc

1. What actually happened: initial documented run.
2. Your move: one consequential choice.
3. Replay receipts: how the result can be inspected.
4. One change, next attempt: hypothesis rather than causal overclaim.
5. Routing: player-visible paths, gated on actual map readiness.
6. Game failure versus infrastructure failure.
7. Expert annotation of a real run.
8. Matched-condition comparison, gated on protocol and integrations.
9. A community-submitted challenge under stated rules.
10. Cost and latency, only with measured compatible data.
11. Contributor improvement and reproducible evidence.
12. Season review with all attempt/intervention accounting.

The schedule must adapt to real availability; do not fake a test to satisfy a content slot. Replace blocked topics with honest build/report material. The watchdog is not a 24/7 claim until real recovery/soak evidence exists.

## Creator and community collaboration

Prepare small, personalized draft outreach with a specific real artifact and a bounded request: expert annotation, a route discussion, or defining a fair comparison. Do not scrape private contacts, bulk-send unsolicited mail, invent a partner agreement, or automatically post into communities. Verify channel rules and permission before publishing. Credit commentary and contributions accurately. Community choice features are participation, not covert vote manipulation.

Define contributor journeys with real good-first tasks, reproducible expected output, code-owner review, and next steps. Stars, follows, and shares are optional. Do not gate artifacts behind engagement demands. Include a report-correction path and moderation guidance for model/provider rivalry.

## Experiments and spending

Define at least six bounded tests: experience-first versus architecture-first intro, outcome versus question title, report versus short clip entry point, decision-card versus generic promo image, developer quickstart variant, and creator-annotated versus unannotated analysis. Specify primary metric, denominator, privacy implications, observation window, minimal useful sample or qualitative protocol, stopping rule, and confounders. Do not prefill wins or invent statistical significance. Treat platform guidance as something to recheck, not a guarantee of reach.

No ads, domain purchases, paid assets, gifts, or creator compensation without explicit budget approval. Sponsorship terms must never promise favorable benchmark outcomes. Disclose material support alongside affected experiments.

## Acceptance

Verify content count and completeness, no unsourced claims, all asset references resolve, all CTAs have real destinations or clearly gated future status, and no generated evidence is presented as gameplay. Real campaign operation is a separate post-launch phase; the deliverable now is a usable operating kit with authority boundaries and measurement definitions.
